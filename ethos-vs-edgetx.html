ETHOS vs EdgeTX: which firmware fits your FrSky radio

ETHOS vs EdgeTX is mostly a hardware question. ETHOS is FrSky's own OS for its newer radios; EdgeTX is an open source firmware for many brands, with older FrSky models on a community supported list.

Choose by radio. On FrSky's newer X20, X18 and X14 families, ETHOS is the only option, since EdgeTX does not list them. On older FrSky radios such as the X9D or QX7, EdgeTX is the option, and FrSky does not list ETHOS for them. The Horus X10 and X12S are the overlap: ETHOS needs the ISRM version, and EdgeTX supports them only through community developers.

ETHOS vs EdgeTX in one paragraph

ETHOS vs EdgeTX is less a feature contest than a question of which radio you hold. ETHOS comes from FrSky, the company that makes the transmitter, the RF modules and the receivers. EdgeTX describes itself as open source transmitter firmware and lists RadioMaster, Jumper, FlySky and HelloRadioSky as official partners. Both offer full model setup, telemetry and Lua; where they differ is who builds them and which hardware they run on.

Where FrSky radios fall

EdgeTX's supported radios page puts every FrSky model under a heading that says the EdgeTX team does not support it; development is by other people in the community. The models listed are the X9D family, X9E, X9 Lite, X10 and X10 Express, X12S and X12S ISRM, the X Lite range and the QX7 family, and version 2.11 is the last EdgeTX release for several of them, including the X9D, X9 Lite and original QX7.

FrSky's own list for ETHOS starts where that one stops: TANDEM radios such as the X20 and X18, TWIN radios such as the X14, and the X10 and X12 with ISRM. The full set is on the radios that run ETHOS page. On an X10 or X12S with ISRM you have a choice; on anything newer you do not.

Receivers, stabilizers and updates

Because FrSky builds ETHOS, its Suite also handles the FrSky parts around the radio: the internal RF module flash, receiver firmware in Download center, over the air updates for TD, TW and ACCESS receivers, and the StabilizerConfig script for ARCHER Plus, TD and TW stabilized receivers. If your fleet is FrSky receivers, that integration is the main reason to stay on ETHOS.

EdgeTX leans the other way: broad protocol support and many radio brands. Its home page mentions that Companion can install the ExpressLRS Lua tool, which fits the RadioMaster and Jumper radios it partners with. Our ETHOS review covers the FrSky side in more detail.

Which to pick

If ETHOS is your answer, the FrSky Suite download page is the starting point.

  • X20, X18, X14 or another TANDEM or TWIN radio: ETHOS, the only listed option
  • X10 or X12S with ISRM and a fleet of FrSky receivers: ETHOS, for the Suite's receiver and RF module tools
  • X10 or X12S and a preference for one firmware across brands: EdgeTX, knowing support is from the community
  • X9D, QX7 or X9 Lite: EdgeTX, with 2.11 as the last version for several of them
  • RadioMaster, Jumper or FlySky radio: EdgeTX

Frequently asked questions

Is ETHOS better than EdgeTX?

Neither wins outright; the radio decides. ETHOS is FrSky's own firmware with tight support for FrSky RF modules and receivers, while EdgeTX runs on many brands. Newer FrSky radios are listed only for ETHOS.

Can I install EdgeTX on a FrSky X20 or X18?

EdgeTX's supported radios page does not list the X20, X18 or X14. Its FrSky entries are older models, all marked as community supported only.

Can I switch my X10 from ETHOS to EdgeTX?

EdgeTX lists the X10, X10S and X12S, including ISRM versions of the X12S, as community supported only. Back up your ETHOS models first, since the two firmwares do not share a documented model format.

Which is free, ETHOS or EdgeTX?

Neither publishes a price. ETHOS downloads are on FrSky's site; EdgeTX is open source and takes donations through OpenCollective.

What is the EdgeTX equivalent of FrSky Suite?

EdgeTX's desktop program is called Companion. FrSky Suite plays that role for ETHOS and also flashes FrSky RF modules and fetches receiver firmware.
